# The Light We Make

"Well here's to a fruitful endeavor for the next few days," I muttered to myself.

It's almost dark when the airship landed. I'm currently here at Driegones for a short business trip. I was sent here by my company to scour the archives for market patterns that could help us sell more of our products back home.

*"Hey Mark, your assignment for today is to go to Aspana. Find us the trick we could use and become rich!"*

I sighed remembering what my lousy, money-grubbing boss said before handing me an airship ticket to Driegones. "Now where do I go and look at the Archives?"

I had always imagined the Great Archive as a library. That was what everyone called it, after all—the greatest collection of knowledge in Aspana. Thousands of years of Witnesses, preserved since before the beginning of our calendars. I expected shelves. Instead, I found a city beneath the city.

Massive walls and corridors of metal and lights. Small beeps emanating everywhere. And among those massive walls are scholars whirring about looking at tiny screens on the wall, each with a pen and paper in their hands. Scholars are the people who study and run the Archives. They're the ones who read and interpret what the Witnesses say; they're basically the smartest and most knowledgeable people here in Rutrinda.

Oh, you're asking what the Witnesses are? They're basically the messages that appear in the Archive every day at dawn. It has been a ritual of the Scholars to wake up early and await the Witness for the day. As for what's in the Witnesses... I have no idea. All I know is that the Scholars read the Witness and interpret them for everyone.

I beelined towards a Scholar to ask for instructions to find what I came here for. "Hey, sorry to bother you, but could you point me towards where I could find market data? Specifically for candles."

"Candles. Really? You went to the Archives just for that?"

"Can't say I volunteered myself for this."

"Go to the Order of Humanity section over there. Look for Adrian." She pointed towards a section near the middle of the Archive.

"Okay, thanks." I gave my gesture of thanks and headed towards where she pointed me.

"If you need data you could travel to Galveros or Esplelasta. You might find other data there although everything funnels here in Driegones," she shouted towards me.

"Okay, thanks. I'll keep that in mind, although I don't have the funds for an airship ticket there."

I arrived at the Order of Humanity. I could see a scholar with a red robe writing vigorously on his pad. I approached him to ask where I could find Adri—

"Hi, I'm Adrian Valdez. You can just call me Adrian. Viola gave me a heads up before you approached me. You're looking for market data, right?" he asked, not looking away from the screen.

Well, that's convenient. "My name's Mark Fletcher. Nice to meet you. And yes, I'm looking for market data about candles."

"Which region are you looking for?"

"Uhh... give me all of Roshea."

He raised an eyebrow, still focused on the screen and not me. "We're very ambitious, I see."

"My boss wants me to see patterns on how people buy candles."

"That's too vague. Narrow it down some more."

"Uhhh... I don't know what you mean..."

"Ugh," he grunted, then looked at me for the first time. "Narrow it down by period to start with. That should be enough for now."

"How long ago do we have records for?"

"About four thousand years. Give or take another thousand."

"Four thousand?! Wha—"

Adrian sighed. "Let's narrow it down to the last 500 years for now," Adrian said while he returned to the screen and started tapping. "Okay, here's the data you were asking for."

Adrian handed me a long list of numbers. "Is this it?"

"You asked for market data. I gave you market data. That's what you asked for, right?"

"Don't you Scholars interpret what all of this means?"

"Ugh. Fine."

Just as Adrian was about to grab the paper he handed me, a loud sound started to ring about in the Archive. "Uhh, what was that?" I asked Adrian.

When I looked at Adrian, his face was frozen still. On the screen in front of us read:

> ANOMALOUS CONDITIONS DETECTED.
> REMAIN WITHIN YOUR CURRENT AREA.
> FURTHER INSTRUCTIONS WILL FOLLOW.

"Hey, uhh Adrian, what does that mean?" No response. "Hey Adrian. Yoohoo. Hello?"

He then jolted alert. "Go find somewhere safe. NOW."

Just as he said that, a proclamation from the High King of Aspana was blared out all over the Archive: *"The Seventh Archive has given us a Warning. I order all civilians to take shelter until further notice. All scholars are expected to remain in premises. All Regional Princes are expected to be present in the emergency council."*

"Get out of here, Mark. You've been instructed to shelter."

"But how about you all? Aren't all of you in danger?" I asked, worried for all the scholars in the Archive.

"Us? In danger? Oh no, no, no. This is the best days of our lives! It's extremely rare for the Archive to give a Warning. We can't let an opportunity like this pass by!"

Bewildered by Adrian's remarks, I stood there stunned. Then I started to hear the other Scholars shout towards the other Orders: "Hey, we need all the Witnesses that we can. Start going through the Archive!" "Which regions did the Seventh relay the Warning?" "Both the northern and southern edge of Rutrinda." "Ask the Order of the Waters for sea level rise." "Hey Order of the Heavens, anything wrong in the sky?" "We need to figure out the impact this would cause to our population."

The whole place was sent into a flurry of activity. Scholars comparing Witnesses, pulling up records from centuries past to see if it had happened before. Some were already debating what the Warning meant; others were just eager to witness something that most Scholars would never see in their lifetime.

"You should leave now, Mark. The place is going to get even more rowdy than it currently is. There's a civilian shelter a few meters outside of the Archive entrance. Stay there until given the okay."

"Alright. Y'all stay safe here, I guess."

"Oh, before I forget," Adrian handed me back the data I asked for. "Sorry I can't help interpret the data for you. So here's the records from until five hundred years ago. You're a smart guy, right? You can figure it out."

"Uhh thanks. Thanks for the help, Adrian. Say thanks to Viola for me too."

I started walking towards the entrance of the Archive. It was only now that I realized how big of a deal this all was. A once in a lifetime moment happening in front of my eyes. But all I could think of was... "Oh god, I should go shelter now."

I was afraid. Fantastic. First trip outside Roshea and I was going to die in a library. What do you mean an "Anomaly" was detected? Why do I have to shelter? Am I in danger? Am I gonna die? Did someone declare war? Is a large storm approaching? Would my boss even send flowers? He wouldn't pay a cent to even buy a single petal.

Those thoughts kept going in circles in my head. In a panic, I started to run towards the other civilians who were trying to enter the shelter. Since everyone was trying to get in, there was a long line that formed outside. "Keep moving! Keep moving!" someone shouted. I pushed my way through the crowd and finally made it inside.

The shelter was just a large stone room with benches lining the walls. People were sitting down, talking amongst themselves about what was happening. "Do you know what the Warning means?" "No idea." "They said it was an anomaly." "Maybe it's another earthquake." "Could be a storm." "Whatever it is, the Archive wouldn't tell us to shelter if it wasn't dangerous."

I sat down and tried to catch my breath. What have I gotten myself into? I should have just stayed in Roshea. I looked around the shelter. Everyone seemed just as confused as I was. But one thing was for certain: I was the only one panicking.

Everyone was calm and collected. Some were discussing what the Anomaly could mean; others were simply waiting patiently. How can they stay calm in a situation like this? "The Archive's never steered us wrong before," someone said a few benches over, like that settled everything. "Never once," someone else agreed, and a few heads nodded along.

Right. Of course. I wasn't the only one panicking because I was more of a coward than the rest of them. I was the only one panicking because I was the only person in that room who hadn't spent their whole life being told the Archive was always right. To them, a Warning wasn't a threat. It was proof the system worked. I was just a Roshean who'd wandered into somebody else's faith.

A few hours passed and then suddenly everything went dark. All the lights in the shelter went poof. I was sure I was going to die. Just as I was about to accept my fate, the door of the shelter opened. On the door stood a Scholar that proclaimed: "The High King has declared it safe to leave the shelters. Electricity and infrastructure would be down for a few hours but everything will be back to normal. The Archive remains operational. Enjoy the lights."

Huh? That's it? No drastic events? No lifethreatening storm overhead? "Enjoy the lights"? What does that mean? I was so confused.

As we were let out of the shelter, I got what he meant. Sprawling across the sky was a faint glow of colors and lights I had never seen before. It was barely noticeable, but it was clearly there. The light looked green and stretched across the sky, twisting and curling like a curtain of light was draped all over the skies of Driegones. The light moved along as if it were being buffeted by the wind. Green slowly turned red. Red became violet. Violet became green again. The colors mixed and rolled across the sky creating one beautiful canvas.

"Apparently that's called an aurora. Oh and your mouth has been open for a while now by the way." I closed my mouth at the remark and I looked towards the origin of the voice and saw Adrian beside me.

"So that's what the warning was for?" I asked him.

"Hmm... not really. It's more about what caused it that the Archive was warning us about. The Order of the Heavens saw spikes of solar radiation right before the Warning was issued so we dug into that some more. Our Order then looked at how this would impact people and found that it would be safe. So the High King rescinded the shelter order."

"So how long would the electric grid be down for?"

"Just a few hours. We turned it off to protect it from damage from solar radiation."

We stared at the aurora for a few more seconds. It was such a mesmerizing sight. "Okay then, I'll get going," Adrian broke the silence between us. "I have to get back to my post."

"Oh, sure. Thanks for letting me know what happened."

"No problem. Oh and by the way, go look around town. You might see something out there."

"Huh?" I quipped, bewildered.

"Seems like today is a good business day for your company. See you," Adrian said while walking back towards the Archive entrance.

I looked around just as he said. I started to notice people holding these small lights with them, or on the counters of shops, or even outside their homes. I stared at them for a moment. Then I looked back at the aurora. "Oh so that's what he meant."

A few days later, I was back in Mightwell, Roshea. The trip to Aspana was over, and I was finally back home. I still had the papers Adrian gave me, but I also had something much more valuable: An idea.

"So let me get this straight. I spent all that money to send you to Aspana and this is all you came up with?" My boss didn't look up from the receipts on his desk.

"Sir, an emergency happened when I was there and that made me realize that candles are a great tool to have during emergencies! We can sell them as 'Emergency Candles' so people have light when there's no electricity!"

He kept sorting through the receipts. "Mark."

"Yes, sir?"

"Those are just normal candles."

I blinked. "What?"

"The candles we already sell. They're the same candles." He still hadn't looked at me once.

"But we can market them as emergency candles."

"We already tell people they can use them when the electricity goes out." He finally glanced up, unimpressed, then went right back to the receipts. "Did you think nobody here had thought of that before you flew halfway across the continent?"

I stood there in silence. "You mean... we already do that?"

"Yes. And that trip's coming out of your paycheck." He didn't look up again.
